# Scene 6 sources — Biblical cosmographie (circular earth, vaulted heauen)

Cipher work is **background only** (Baconian biliteral of the five appointed sentences). The main figure is a period-correct diagram of the first creation: a circular earth, ocean, four winds, firmament, waters above.

Do not introduce globes, orreries of spheres, or NASA-style ice-wall diagrams. Stay with 16th–17th-century English / late-medieval Christian chart language.

## Primary printed and manuscript types

1. **Genesis creation woodcuts** in English Bibles (Coverdale 1535, Great Bible 1539, Geneva 1560, Bishops’ 1568, Authorised 1611 title-page and Genesis headpieces). Disk of land and sea under a scalloped heaven; sun, moon, stars set in the firmament.
2. **Isaiah 40:22 diagrams** used in sermons and commonplace books: “circle of the earth”, heavens stretched “as a curtaine” / “as a tent”.
3. **T–O mappae mundi** (Isidore of Seville tradition, printed in 16th-c. English editions of the *Etymologiae*): circular earth, east at top or east marked, Asia / Europe / Africa, ocean ring. Jerusalem often toward centre in later Christian copies.
4. **Hereford Cathedral mappa mundi** (c. 1300) and printed reductions: circular world, paradise in the east, surrounding ocean — use as *structure*, not as a copy.
5. **Cosmas Indicopleustes, Christian Topography** (Greek; Latin/printed extracts known to early-modern scholars): vaulted heaven over a planar earth, tabernacle as the pattern of the world. Rectangular earth under a lid; we use the *vault + plane* idea, drawn as a circular “circle of the earth” to match Isaiah 40:22 as read in this project.
6. **English cosmographical teaching cuts** — Thomas Blundeville *Exercises* (1594) diagrams of the heavens as a covering; treatises that still draw the firmament as an arch over the inhabited earth even while discussing spheres. Take the **arch-over-disk** plates, not the nested-globe plates.
7. **Volvelles in English almanacs** (Compost of Ptolemeus tradition, perpetual almanacs printed in London): rotating disks for sun and moon over a **plan** of the year/winds — used here as sunne/moone travelling the vault, not as a terrestrial globe.
8. **Four winds / four corners** woodcuts in Bibles and emblem books (Rev. 7:1, Jer. 49:36): angel or head at N E S W of a circular earth.
9. **Waters above the firmament** — Genesis 1:6–8 as printed 1611; diagram form is a band or second heaven above the starry vault (common in medieval Genesis cycles and some 16th-c. Bible illustration).
10. **Joseph Moxon / English globe-makers** — **do not use** their spherical earth. Listed so the renderer knows to avoid that language.

## Baconian (background only)

- Francis Bacon, *De augmentis scientiarum* (1623), the biliteral cipher (A/B, two alphabets / two typefaces).
- Render as faint A/B ticks or two-weight marks, never as the main composition.
- Phrases (as given, not re-paraphrased):  
  *Jesus is God* · *Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ and though shalt be saved* · *Do not be deceived* · *Satan himself is transformed into an angel of light* · the John 8:44 sentence on the devil as liar and murderer.

## Colour

Gemstone palettes only. Dark crystal / chalcedony / sapphire well. Amber and pearl for sunne, moone, labels.

## Motion

Sunne travels the vault by day, moone by night. Stars on the firmament breathe. Baconian field stays slow. Disk of the earth does not spin like a globe.
